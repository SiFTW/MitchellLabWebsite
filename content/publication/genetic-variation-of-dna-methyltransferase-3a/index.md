abstract: "The role of the host in development of persistent methicillin-resistant\n\
  \ *Staphylococcus aureus*\n (MRSA) bacteremia is not well understood. A cohort of\
  \ prospectively enrolled patients with persistent methicillin-resistant\n *S. aureus*\n\
  \ bacteremia (PB) and resolving methicillin-resistant\n *S. aureus*\n bacteremia\
  \ (RB) matched by sex, age, race, hemodialysis status, diabetes mellitus, and presence\
  \ of implantable medical device was studied to gain insights into this question.\
  \ One heterozygous g.25498283A > C polymorphism located in the\n *DNMT3A*\n intronic\
  \ region of chromosome 2p with no impact in messenger RNA (mRNA) expression was\
  \ more common in RB (21 of 34, 61.8%) than PB (3 of 34, 8.8%) patients (\n *P*\n\
  \ = 7.8 × 10\n −6 \n ). Patients with MRSA bacteremia and g.25498283A > C genotype\
  \ exhibited significantly higher levels of methylation in gene-regulatory CpG island\
  \ regions (Δmethylation = 4.1%,\n *P*\n C genotype within\n *DNMT3A*\n contributes\
  \ to increased capacity to resolve MRSA bacteremia, potentially through a mechanism\
  \ involving increased methylation of gene-regulatory regions and reduced levels\
  \ of antiinflammatory cytokine IL-10."

abstract: "<jats:p>\n                    The role of the host in development of persistent\
  \ methicillin-resistant\n                    <jats:italic>Staphylococcus aureus</jats:italic>\n\
  \                    (MRSA) bacteremia is not well understood. A cohort of prospectively\
  \ enrolled patients with persistent methicillin-resistant\n                    <jats:italic>S.\
  \ aureus</jats:italic>\n                    bacteremia (PB) and resolving methicillin-resistant\n\
  \                    <jats:italic>S. aureus</jats:italic>\n                    bacteremia\
  \ (RB) matched by sex, age, race, hemodialysis status, diabetes mellitus, and presence\
  \ of implantable medical device was studied to gain insights into this question.\
  \ One heterozygous g.25498283A &gt; C polymorphism located in the\n            \
  \        <jats:italic>DNMT3A</jats:italic>\n                    intronic region\
  \ of chromosome 2p with no impact in messenger RNA (mRNA) expression was more common\
  \ in RB (21 of 34, 61.8%) than PB (3 of 34, 8.8%) patients (\n                 \
  \   <jats:italic>P</jats:italic>\n                    = 7.8 \xD7 10\n          \
  \          <jats:sup>\u22126</jats:sup>\n                    ). Patients with MRSA\
  \ bacteremia and g.25498283A &gt; C genotype exhibited significantly higher levels\
  \ of methylation in gene-regulatory CpG island regions (\u0394methylation = 4.1%,\n\
  \                    <jats:italic>P</jats:italic>\n                    &lt; 0.0001)\
  \ and significantly lower serum levels of interleukin-10 (IL-10) than patients with\
  \ MRSA bacteremia without\n                    <jats:italic>DNMT3A</jats:italic>\n\
  \                    mutation (A/C: 9.7038 pg/mL vs. A/A: 52.9898 pg/mL;\n     \
  \               <jats:italic>P</jats:italic>\n                    = 0.0042). Expression\
  \ of\n                    <jats:italic>DNMT3A</jats:italic>\n                  \
  \  was significantly suppressed in patients with\n                    <jats:italic>S.\
  \ aureus</jats:italic>\n                    bacteremia and in\n                \
  \    <jats:italic>S. aureus</jats:italic>\n                    -challenged primary\
  \ human macrophages. Small interfering RNA (siRNA) silencing of\n              \
  \      <jats:italic>DNMT3A</jats:italic>\n                    expression in human\
  \ macrophages caused increased IL-10 response upon\n                    <jats:italic>S.\
  \ aureus</jats:italic>\n                    stimulation. Treating macrophages with\
  \ methylation inhibitor 5-Aza-2\u2032-deoxycytidine resulted in increased levels\
  \ of IL-10 when challenged with\n                    <jats:italic>S. aureus</jats:italic>\n\
  \                    . In the murine sepsis model, methylation inhibition increased\
  \ susceptibility to\n                    <jats:italic>S. aureus</jats:italic>\n\
  \                    . These findings indicate that g.25498283A &gt; C genotype\
  \ within\n                    <jats:italic>DNMT3A</jats:italic>\n              \
  \      contributes to increased capacity to resolve MRSA bacteremia, potentially\
  \ through a mechanism involving increased methylation of gene-regulatory regions\
  \ and reduced levels of antiinflammatory cytokine IL-10.\n                  </jats:p>"

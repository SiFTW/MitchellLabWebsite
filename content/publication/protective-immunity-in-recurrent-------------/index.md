abstract: "<jats:title>Significance</jats:title>\n                  <jats:p>\n   \
  \                 <jats:italic>Staphylococcus aureus</jats:italic>\n           \
  \         (SA) is the leading cause of skin and skin structure infections (SSSI),\
  \ which are the main portal of entry for life-threatening invasive infections. Treatment\
  \ failures are increasingly common due to antibiotic resistance. Importantly, SA\
  \ SSSI exhibit high 1-year recurrence, despite high antibody levels against staphylococcal\
  \ antigens. Therefore, critical determinants of immune protection against recurrent\
  \ SA SSSI are not well understood. The present study offers important insights in\
  \ this area, including: (\n                    <jats:italic>i</jats:italic>\n  \
  \                  ) macrophages can confer protective memory transferrable to na\xEF\
  ve hosts; (\n                    <jats:italic>ii</jats:italic>\n               \
  \     ) SA SSSI can induce memory in bone marrow myeloid precursors; and (\n   \
  \                 <jats:italic>iii</jats:italic>\n                    ) specific\
  \ memory against recurring SA SSSI is localized and distinct from disseminated infection.\
  \ These findings will accelerate novel strategies to prevent and treat SA infections.\n\
  \                  </jats:p>"
